# Coaching and Mentoring Framework

## Purpose

The coaching framework helps Centre Directors and Area Managers turn insight into supported, sustainable improvement. It creates a regular space for strengths, reflection, goals, commitments, and help—not a hidden disciplinary record.

Coaching may connect to operational work by agreement, but operational findings, corrective actions, and performance processes retain their own governed workflows.

## Principles

- Strengths-based and improvement-oriented.
- Clear purpose and participant expectations at the start of a cycle.
- Centre Director voice and psychological safety.
- Minimal, agreed record rather than verbatim surveillance.
- Confidentiality boundaries explained before capture.
- Actions are realistic, owned, supported, and reviewed.
- Audit/Centre Health signals prompt inquiry, not predetermined judgement.
- No identifiable wellbeing response is used as coaching content.

## Coaching cycle

### 1. Establish

Record participants, purpose, expected duration, cadence, confidentiality level, cancellation/transfer process, and agreed outcomes. A coaching relationship does not itself grant broader centre access.

### 2. Understand

Review participant-selected context, strengths, relevant goals, and authorised operational signals. Use reflective questions rather than auto-generated conclusions.

### 3. Plan

Create a small number of goals with desired outcome, measures, milestones, support, review date, and permitted links to QIP/actions.

### 4. Meet and reflect

Capture an agreed summary, progress, strengths, decisions, commitments, and support needs. Participants should see what will be retained and who can access it.

### 5. Review

Assess whether outcomes improved, support remains appropriate, and goals should continue, change, complete, or move to an operational workflow.

### 6. Close or renew

Close with a shared outcome summary and retention rule. A new cycle creates a new purpose rather than silently extending access.

Cycle state: `proposed -> active -> paused -> completed/cancelled`, with participant-visible reasons for pause/cancellation.

## Goal and session model

A goal includes:

- participant-defined outcome and success evidence;
- relationship to a centre/QIP/action only if relevant and permitted;
- owner and supporter;
- target/review dates;
- milestones and resources; and
- visibility level.

A session record includes date, participants, agreed summary, strengths, commitments, next meeting, and visibility. Personal scratch notes are not stored as an undisclosed organisational record.

## Confidentiality levels

Exact policy requires Bright Steps approval. The design supports:

- **Shared participants:** visible only to named participants.
- **Shared operational:** agreed summary/actions visible to defined centre or specialist roles.
- **Restricted referral:** minimal referral record visible only to the authorised recipient and participants as policy permits.

Executives and Compliance Managers do not receive participant-level coaching notes by default. They may receive safe aggregate programme measures. System administrators do not gain content access.

Confidentiality cannot promise secrecy where Bright Steps has an approved obligation to act. Any exception and escalation path must be explained in advance and validated by appropriate legal/people owners.

## Connections to other domains

- **Internal audit:** a result can suggest a coaching conversation; only authorised facts are linked. The audit itself remains immutable.
- **Corrective action:** coaching can provide support, but verified closure occurs in the action workflow.
- **QIP:** agreed goals can contribute to a QIP improvement with participant awareness.
- **Centre Health:** only operational commitments explicitly shared for that purpose may contribute; private notes never do.
- **Recognition:** participants can approve a strength/achievement for broader recognition.
- **Wellbeing:** aggregate wellbeing can inform general support planning, never individual coaching inference.

## Prompts and AI assistance

Approved prompts may help prepare reflective questions, summarise participant-approved notes, draft goals, or suggest resources from the governed knowledge base. AI must:

- use only participant-authorised context;
- label suggestions and cite organisational guidance where relevant;
- avoid diagnoses, personality judgements, disciplinary recommendations, or inferred wellbeing;
- not access private notes or raw wellbeing responses; and
- require participant review before saving.

## Programme oversight

Appropriate aggregate measures may include cycle uptake, cadence, participant-rated usefulness, goal progress, support-request themes at safe aggregation, and shared recognition. Do not rank coaches or Centre Directors by note volume, meeting frequency, or goal closure alone.

## Open decisions

- Bright Steps coaching methodology and owner.
- Confidentiality policy, disclosure exceptions, and retention.
- Whether external mentors participate and how access expires.
- Which operational links are visible to whom.
- Participant correction/export rights.
- AI use and approved resource library.
